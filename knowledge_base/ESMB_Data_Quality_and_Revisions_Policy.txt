EUROPEAN STATISTICAL METHODS BOARD

ESMB DATA QUALITY AND REVISIONS POLICY

Version 3.0. Adopted by the Plenary on 20 June 2024. Replaces version 2.2 of 2019.


1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1 Purpose
This policy sets out the quality framework and the revision rules that apply to
all statistics compiled under ESMB standards by the Member Statistical
Authorities (MSAs). It aims to ensure that users can judge the fitness for
purpose of each statistic and can understand why and when published figures
change.

1.2 Scope
The policy covers official statistics produced by MSAs and transmitted under
ESMB regulations, including price statistics, labour market statistics,
national accounts and regional statistics. It also applies to the derived
indicators and commentary that accompany those statistics.


2. QUALITY DIMENSIONS

2.1 Framework
The quality of a statistic is assessed along the five dimensions set out below.
Each MSA shall publish a quality report for every statistical domain at least
every three years, structured along these dimensions.

2.2 Relevance
The degree to which statistics meet current and potential needs of users.
MSAs shall consult users regularly and document the concepts, definitions and
classifications used, including any departure from ESMB standards.

2.3 Accuracy and reliability
Accuracy is the closeness of an estimate to the unknown true value. It is
affected by sampling errors and by non-sampling errors such as coverage,
measurement, non-response and processing errors. Reliability is the closeness
of the first published estimate to subsequent estimates of the same quantity.
It is measured through revision analysis (Section 5).

2.4 Timeliness and punctuality
Timeliness is the time between the end of the reference period and the
publication of the statistic. Punctuality is the time lag between the actual
and the announced release date. MSAs shall publish release calendars in
advance and report any deviation from them.

2.5 Coherence and comparability
Coherence is the extent to which statistics from different sources or of
different frequency can be reliably combined. Comparability covers comparison
over time and across regions and countries. Differences in definitions,
base years, units, seasonal adjustment or territorial classification shall be
documented next to the data. Series that differ in any of these respects shall
not be combined without explicit conversion.

2.6 Accessibility and clarity
Statistics shall be available in machine-readable form with metadata that
explain concepts, units, sources and methods. Every column or variable in a
disseminated file shall be defined in the accompanying metadata. Variables for
which no definition exists shall not be disseminated as official statistics.


3. REVISIONS

3.1 Principles
Revisions are a normal consequence of the statistical process. They improve
accuracy as more complete information becomes available. MSAs shall follow
the principles of transparency (revisions are announced and explained),
predictability (routine revisions follow a published timetable) and
consistency (revised data are released for all affected periods and series
at the same time).

3.2 Routine revisions
Routine revisions are scheduled revisions that incorporate late or more
complete source data, updated seasonal factors or revised weights. Examples
are the revision of the previous month's provisional regional indicators in
the following monthly release and the incorporation of annual data in
quarterly national accounts.

3.3 Major revisions
Major (benchmark) revisions follow changes in concepts, definitions,
classifications, methods, base years or reference years. They are announced at
least three months in advance, together with an explanation of their expected
impact, and are accompanied by complete back series.

3.4 Error corrections
Errors discovered after publication shall be corrected as soon as possible. The
correction shall be announced on the dissemination page of the statistic,
stating the periods and series affected, the nature of the error and the size
of the correction. Corrections are not scheduled and are distinct from routine
revisions.

3.5 Rebasing
A change of index reference period (base year) is a major revision in form but
not in substance: index levels change while rates of change remain the same
except for rounding. Rebased series shall carry the new base in the unit of
measure, for example "index, 2021=100", so that series on different bases
cannot be confused.


4. FLAGS AND SPECIAL VALUES

4.1 Observation flags
The following flags shall be attached to observations where applicable:

  Flag   Meaning
  ----   -------------------------------------------------------------
  p      provisional value, expected to be revised
  e      estimated value
  b      break in time series
  c      confidential value, not published
  u      low reliability
  d      definition differs from the standard definition
  :      not available

4.2 Missing values
A missing value shall be left empty or shown with the symbol ":" and shall
never be replaced by zero. A zero means that the quantity is zero or rounds to
zero.

4.3 Rounding
Published values are rounded independently. Totals may therefore differ from
the sum of their rounded components. Rates of change shall be computed from
unrounded or less rounded data and not from the rounded published values,
unless a regulation prescribes otherwise.


5. REVISION ANALYSIS

5.1 Vintages
MSAs shall keep a record of every published version (vintage) of their
principal series so that revisions can be analysed and users can reproduce
earlier analyses.

5.2 Measures
The following measures shall be computed at least annually for each principal
indicator, comparing first published and later estimates:
  - mean revision (average of later minus first estimate), an indicator of bias;
  - mean absolute revision, an indicator of the size of revisions;
  - the share of revisions that change the sign of a growth rate.

5.3 Interpretation by users
A change between two periods that is smaller than the mean absolute revision
of the indicator should be described with caution, because it may disappear
when the data are revised.


6. METADATA AND DOCUMENTATION

6.1 Reference metadata
Each dataset shall be accompanied by reference metadata describing the
contact organisation, concepts and definitions, statistical population,
reference period, unit of measure, base period, source data, frequency,
timeliness, revision policy and quality management.

6.2 Undocumented content
Where a file received from a third party contains variables that are not
described in its metadata, the producer or user shall treat those variables as
of unknown meaning. They may be listed, but no statistical interpretation shall
be attached to them until their definition has been obtained from the data
provider.


7. ENTRY INTO FORCE

This policy enters into force on 1 September 2024. MSAs shall publish their
first quality reports under version 3.0 no later than 31 December 2025.
