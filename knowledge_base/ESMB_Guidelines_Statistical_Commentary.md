# ESMB Guidelines for Statistical Commentary

European Statistical Methods Board. Guidelines G-2023-02, version 1.0, adopted on 3 April 2023. These guidelines apply to the written commentary that accompanies statistical releases, reports and dashboards produced under ESMB standards.

## 1 Purpose and principles

### 1.1 Purpose

Statistical commentary helps users understand what the numbers show. It must be accurate, impartial and clear, and it must not say more than the data support. These guidelines set common wording rules so that commentary is consistent across statistical domains and producers.

### 1.2 Principles

Commentary shall be:

- **impartial**: it describes developments without praising or criticising policies, institutions or persons;
- **evidence-based**: every statement can be traced to a published figure or a cited source;
- **proportionate**: the emphasis given to a change reflects its size and its statistical significance;
- **transparent**: limitations, provisional status and breaks in series are stated where they affect the interpretation.

## 2 Describing change

### 2.1 Percent and percentage points

A change in a quantity measured in percent, such as an inflation rate, an unemployment rate or a share, is expressed in percentage points. If the unemployment rate rises from 5.0 % to 5.5 %, it has increased by 0.5 percentage points. Describing this as an increase of 0.5 % is wrong; in relative terms it is an increase of 10 %. The abbreviation "pp" may be used in tables but should be written in full in running text.

### 2.2 Rates of change

The period of comparison shall always be stated: "compared with the previous month", "compared with the same month of the previous year" or "annual average". The annual rate of inflation and the monthly rate of change shall not be confused. A fall in the annual inflation rate means that prices are rising more slowly, not that prices are falling ("disinflation" rather than "deflation").

### 2.3 Size of changes

Commentary shall distinguish between changes that are large relative to the usual variability and revisions of a series and changes that are not. Changes smaller than the sampling error or the mean absolute revision of an indicator shall be described as "broadly stable" or "little changed". Words such as "surge", "plunge", "soar" or "collapse" shall not be used.

### 2.4 Records and superlatives

A value may be described as the highest or lowest "since" a given date only if this has been verified against the complete published series, and the start of the series shall be mentioned. "Record" shall be avoided unless the full history of the series has been checked.

## 3 Causality and explanation

### 3.1 No causal claims without evidence

Commentary shall not state or imply that one development caused another unless the cause is established by the statistics themselves or by a cited source. Two series moving together is not evidence of a causal link. Wording such as "due to", "because of", "driven by" or "as a result of" is reserved for:

- accounting relationships that follow from the compilation, for example contributions of components to an aggregate ("food prices contributed 1.2 percentage points to the annual rate");
- causes documented in a cited source, such as a change in tax rates recorded in the constant-tax index or a legal change affecting a register.

In all other cases, commentary shall describe co-movement neutrally ("unemployment fell while employment rose") or not at all.

### 3.2 Speculation and forecasts

Commentary on official statistics shall not speculate about future developments or about reasons that are not documented. Forecasts made by other organisations may be quoted only with attribution.

## 4 Numbers, rounding and units

### 4.1 Rounding

Figures in commentary shall be rounded to the precision at which they are published, normally one decimal place for rates and percentages. Spurious precision ("an increase of 4.6273 %") shall be avoided. Changes shall be computed from published or unrounded data, not from figures that were rounded for the text, and the text shall be consistent with the published tables.

### 4.2 Units and base years

The unit of every figure shall be stated. For indices, the base year (index reference period) shall be stated, for example "index, 2021=100". Index levels on different bases shall not be compared in commentary; rates of change may be compared.

### 4.3 Comparing like with like

Commentary shall compare only series that measure the same concept, on the same unit, for the same territorial level and with the same seasonal adjustment status. Where sources differ, for example registered unemployment and unemployment based on the Labour Force Survey, the commentary shall name both concepts and shall not present them as one indicator.

### 4.4 Data of unknown meaning

Variables whose definition is not documented in the available metadata shall not be interpreted. Commentary may mention that such variables exist and that their meaning is unknown, but it shall not assign them a meaning from their name, their values or their correlation with other variables.

## 5 Data quality statements

### 5.1 Provisional data and revisions

When commentary refers to provisional figures, it shall say so. Where a revision changes the picture given in an earlier release, the commentary shall mention the revision explicitly.

### 5.2 Breaks and conflicts between sources

Breaks in series and conflicting values from different sources shall be reported, with the reason where it is known. Commentary shall not silently choose one value when sources disagree.

## 6 Sources and citations

### 6.1 Citation format

Every figure shall be attributable to a source. Commentary shall cite the producer, the dataset or document, the reference period and, for online data, the date of extraction. Methodological statements shall cite the document and the section or page on which they rely.

### 6.2 Examples

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Inflation rose by 0.4 % to 5.1 %. | The annual inflation rate rose by 0.4 percentage points to 5.1 %. |
| Unemployment plunged in Cluj thanks to new investment. | The registered unemployment rate in Cluj fell from 2.6 % to 2.4 %. |
| Prices are 23 points higher in the European series. | The two indices use different base years (2015=100 and 2021=100); their annual rates are 6.7 % and 6.6 %. |
| GDP grew 2.4718 %. | GDP increased by 2.5 % in volume terms compared with the previous year. |
| The column Q17 shows adjusted values. | The meaning of the column Q17 is not documented. |
